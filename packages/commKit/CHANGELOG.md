# Changelog

## 0.3.0 — 2026-09-25

Contract change, approved by the owner: **a send made while the driver is idle leaves inside `Send`**, as ChatThrottleLib sends at once while bandwidth is left. The wire protocol and API generation 1 are unchanged; peers on revision 5 and earlier interoperate.

- **Sending at once.** When no driver run is on the stack and no job is scheduled (other than one that only makes held callbacks), `Send` runs the driver once on the caller's stack. The run is an ordinary one: it serves the queue in rotation and stops at the bucket, the queue or the 32-chunk cap, so a message the bucket pays for leaves before `Send` returns and builds no SchedulerKit job, one it pays for in part comes back `sending` and continues from the job, and one it cannot pay for comes back `queued`. A send made while the driver is busy queues as before. `Send` may therefore return a handle already `sending` or `sent`; a throttle or refusal answered inside the call returns it `queued` or `failed`.
- **No callback before `Send` returns.** The progress and completion calls of chunks sent inside `Send` are held in one array in the state (four slots a call, no table each) and made by the next driver run, an immediate `Schedule` job that makes them before it sends anything; a send from a held callback queues and leaves in that run. A `Cancel`, `CancelAll`, `Close` or `CloseAddonScopes` that completes a message while calls are held makes them first, inside that call, so each handle's calls keep their order and none is lost at logout. A held callback whose error escapes the host's `securecallfunction` is reported and cannot leave the driver unable to send at once. SyncSet replies, which count their bytes after `Send` returns and release them in their completion, keep their accounting.
- **Frame-rate sampling.** A wake from idle samples `GetFramerate` only when the last sample is at least a second old, so sends that each leave inside `Send` sample once a second between them rather than once each.
- **Queue records on CommKit's own free list.** Queue records (messages and aborts) come from `state.spares.records`, at most 64, instead of a PoolKit pool: PoolKit's generic lease and reset took about a tenth of a short send's time. A released record lets go of its text, handle, scope, callbacks and pipe at once. A record a revision 5 copy leased goes back to that copy's pool. `docs/API.md` said "leased from a pool" and now names the free list; reassembly streams keep their PoolKit pool.
- `docs/API.md`: the `Send` row, "Send states" (handles returned `sending` or `sent`, the held callbacks), "The driver", the frame-rate sampling sentence, the cost of a send, the lane deviation and "Upgrades". `docs/INTERNALS.md`: the new state fields, records, held callbacks, `runInline` and the driver diagram, and the local-variable budget (seven file-scope locals folded into `do` blocks, six added; peak 199).
- Host benchmark, `python3 -m tooling.bench.run --workload comm --rounds 3` on an Apple M5 Pro under concurrent load (load average 2.4 before, 6.5 after, while other packages were benchmarked; the sides are interleaved, so the ratios are the comparable figures). Before: CommKit revision 5 with SchedulerKit 17, TimerKit 10, EventKit 17, PoolKit 8; after: revision 6 with SchedulerKit 18, TimerKit 11, EventKit 18, PoolKit 9, which other work changed in between. Ratios are CommKit time over AceComm time; allocation is deterministic and includes the benchmark's own request table.

  | Case | Before | After | Allocation before → after (Ace3) | Frames per message |
  |---|---:|---:|---|---|
  | `send-200` | 19.9× (14.4 µs) | 7.8× (5.7 µs) | 4,778 → 834 B (104 B) | 1 → 0 |
  | `send-1000` | 8.7× (25.2 µs) | 5.6× (16.3 µs) | 6,437 → 2,493 B (2,086 B) | 1 → 0 |
  | `send-8000` | 5.0× (114 µs) | 4.5× (105 µs) | 23,223 → 19,276 B (21,679 B) | 1 → 0 |
  | `receive-200` | 3.2× | 3.3× | 225 → 225 B (0 B) | |

  Of the `send-200` gain, running the driver inside `Send` took it from 14.4 to 6.6 µs and the record free list from 6.6 to 5.8 µs, in runs of the same session. The receive path is unchanged; its ratios moved with the other Kits and the load.
- What remains on a short send: the handle the caller keeps (384 B on a 64-bit build) and the chunk string are its only allocations; the time is request validation, the bucket and rotation bookkeeping, the client-result classification per chunk, and looking HookKit up in Registry on every wake from idle until it is found (about 0.5 µs a send; `docs/API.md` promises the hooks the first time the driver wakes after HookKit is found, so the lookup is not rate-limited).
- Implementation revision 6. The state gains `spares.records`, `deferred`, `driver.inline` and `budget.sampledAt`, added by an upgrade over revision 5; a new state creates no `records` pool. `Bootstrap_spec.lua` upgrades a state in revision 5's layout (records leased from a PoolKit pool) with a send in flight, and the revision 4 upgrade spec also rewinds these fields.
- 227 specs: `SendAtOnce_spec.lua` pins sending inside `Send` (in full, in part, not at all, while busy, after a throttle or a refusal), that no callback runs before `Send` returns, the order of held calls through `Cancel` and `Close`, sends from held callbacks, an escaping callback error, the frame-rate sampling, and the record free list; `Allocation_spec.lua` guards that a send the bucket pays for at once allocates only its handle and its chunk string. Queue, priority, lifecycle and robustness specs that need several messages queued at once hold them behind an empty bucket (`CommKitTestEnv.HoldSends` and `ReleaseSends`).
- Real-client suite (`tests/client/MoltenCodesTest_CommKit`): the short round trip now checks that the message left inside `Send` and that `onComplete` had not run when `Send` returned; the tests that need a message to wait in the queue (the facade handle, the abort after the first chunk, the cancelled send, `CloseAddonScopes`, the query allocation guard) make it from the completion callback of one `hold` whisper to the player's own character, which CommKit makes while its driver runs. A run sends at most 25 addon messages (was 20); `EXPECTED.md` gives the two renamed test lines and a "Waiting in the queue" section. Not yet run in a client.

## 0.2.4 — 2026-09-25

Performance round against AceComm-3.0 with ChatThrottleLib. The wire protocol, the API and every documented bound are unchanged; peers on revision 4 and earlier interoperate.

- **Frame-rate ticker started only when needed.** Waking the driver from idle still samples `GetFramerate` once, but the one-second TimerKit ticker is now started by the first run that leaves something queued instead of by every wake. A message the first run sends in full (the usual case) no longer builds and cancels a repeating timer. `docs/API.md` states when the ticker starts.
- **Pipes and per-sender records recycled.** An emptied pipe (two tables) and a sender's reassembly record go to bounded free lists (`state.spares`, 16 each) and are reset on reuse, so a send to an idle destination and a stream from a sender with nothing open build no table.
- **One expiry timer restarted.** Arming a full reassembly timeout, as the first stream to open does, restarts one kept TimerKit timer (`expiry.idleTimer`) instead of building a timer handle per stream; an earlier deadline still gets a one-shot timer, and the timer still runs only while a stream is open.
- **One pooled table per stream.** A stream record keeps its chunks in its own array part instead of a second pooled list, halving the PoolKit leases and releases per stream.
- **Cheaper hot paths.** The send, driver and receive paths index CommKit's own tables directly instead of through `rawget`/`rawset` (none has a metatable); `issecretvalue` is read once per call rather than once per value checked; reassembly keys end in a precomputed per-stream-id suffix instead of formatting the id with `sprintf` on every chunk; a run on an emptied queue ends without walking the priority rotation; the head of a pipe and the oldest stream are removed without a search.
- Host benchmark, `python3 -m tooling.bench.run --workload comm --rounds 3` on an Apple M5 Pro under concurrent load (load average about 6, other packages benchmarked at the same time; the sides are interleaved, so the ratios are the comparable figures). Before: CommKit revision 4 with EventKit 16, SignalKit 8, TimerKit 9; after: revision 5 with EventKit 17, SignalKit 9, TimerKit 10, which other work changed in between. Ratios are CommKit time over AceComm time; allocation is deterministic.

  | Case | Before | After | Allocation before → after (Ace3) |
  |---|---:|---:|---|
  | `send-200` | 29.6× | 19.0× | 6,314 → 4,778 B (104 B) |
  | `send-1000` | 12.7× | 9.3× | 7,973 → 6,437 B (2,086 B) |
  | `send-8000` | 6.4× | 4.9× | 24,761 → 23,223 B (21,679 B) |
  | `receive-200` | 5.8× | 3.3× | 225 → 225 B (0 B) |
  | `receive-1000` | 8.0× | 4.5× | 2,864 → 2,014 B (1,638 B) |
  | `receive-8000` | 4.7× | 2.6× | 31,050 → 30,188 B (30,372 B) |

  Memory an incomplete stream holds: 2,105 → 1,257 B for a 1000-byte message (Ace3 1,023 B), 9,396 → 8,548 B for 8000 bytes (Ace3 8,845 B). Measured with the other Kits held at their current revisions, revision 5 alone takes 28 % off `send-200`, 23 % off `send-1000` and `send-8000`, and 30 to 35 % off every receive case (the same host, one process per case).
- What remains is paid outside CommKit's own code: one SchedulerKit job (its handle, context and coroutine, about 4 KB) each time the driver wakes from idle, which is also why a send takes one frame where ChatThrottleLib sends at once; the send handle the caller keeps (384 B); PoolKit's lease and release of the queue record and the stream record; TimerKit's start of the expiry timer; and EventKit's and SignalKit's isolated dispatch of every chunk and message. The control byte every message carries costs one substring per received single chunk (225 B for 200 bytes), which AceComm, sending single messages unmarked, does not pay.
- Implementation revision 5. The state gains `spares` and `expiry.idleTimer`, added empty by an upgrade over revision 4; a stream a revision 4 copy opened keeps its separate `parts` list until it closes, and a new state creates no `parts` pool. `Bootstrap_spec.lua` upgrades a state in revision 4's layout with a send and a stream in flight.
- 208 specs; `FastPaths_spec.lua` pins each change to the behaviour it keeps (pipe reset, free-list bounds, ticker start and stop, expiry at the timeout and after a timeout change, every stream id of both digit schemes, out-of-order completion), `Allocation_spec.lua` guards that a two-chunk message from a new sender allocates only its three strings, and `ErrorLevels_spec.lua` covers the rewritten prefix checks.

## 0.2.3 — 2026-09-24

- Secret values: the source comments on limit validation, send-request fields and `SetLimits` no longer claim that comparing a secret with anything, `nil` included, raises, or that a raw identity test is safe whatever the other side. They state what was measured on Retail 12.1.0 b69933 (2026-09-24): a secret compared with a value of its own type raises (`==`, `~=`, `<`, `<=` and `rawequal` alike) and a secret used as a table key raises, while a comparison with `nil` or with a value of another type answers without raising. The `type(value) == "nil"` rule stays, as the repository's uniform rule that never compares anything. Comments and documentation only: `luac -s -l` gives the same instruction listing before and after, so the implementation revision is unchanged.

## 0.2.2 — 2026-09-24

- Values CommKit did not create are no longer compared before `issecretvalue` is asked about them, so a secret raises nowhere inside CommKit. Absence of a caller's or a host's value is tested with `type` (options, the `maxRegistrations` repeat check, the `maxReassemblyBytesPerSender` bound, a send target, a `nil` passed to `SyncSet:Set`, the group-membership answer, delivered SyncSet fields). A facade method called with a secret receiver raises its ordinary receiver error at the caller.
- Host and package returns are asked about first: a secret send result is a throttle (as a result the enum does not name already was), a secret `RegisterAddonMessagePrefix` result refuses the prefix as `"unknownResult"`, a secret `IsAddonMessagePrefixRegistered` answer is not taken as registered, a secret entry of the client's result enums gives way to CommKit's own value, a secret SchemaKit verdict is not an acceptance, and a secret entry of LifecycleKit's `CLOSES_ADDON_SCOPES` is not a hand-over. `docs/API.md` lists these under "Secret values". Every value accepted before is still accepted.
- Implementation revision 4. The state layout and scope layout 2 are unchanged; a revision 4 copy inherits everything a revision 3 copy built. `Bootstrap_spec.lua` loads a revision 3 copy with a send in flight, upgrades it and checks the facade, state and prototype identity.
- 195 specs; `SecretValues_spec.lua` covers each of the outcomes above.

## 0.2.0 — 2026-09-23

- CommKit addon scopes are now closed at logout whatever revisions are paired, and an addon without LifecycleKit no longer closes its scope itself. `CommKit:ForAddon(addonName)` finds LifecycleKit with `Registry:Find` and leaves the scope to a LifecycleKit whose `CLOSES_ADDON_SCOPES` names `commKit` (making sure the addon has a LifecycleKit instance), subscribes once to an older LifecycleKit's `OnShutdown`, or, without LifecycleKit, connects one package-level `PLAYER_LOGOUT` watcher in CommKit's own EventKit scope; EventKit is required, so that case is always available. The decision is taken again by later `ForAddon` calls until LifecycleKit has taken the scope over. See "At logout" in `docs/API.md`.
- `Close` and `CloseAddonScopes` disconnect the `OnShutdown` subscription of a scope that closes before logout.
- LifecycleKit API 1 is declared under `optionalDependencies` (a cycle through an optional edge, which the manifest rules allow); CommKit's six required dependencies are unchanged.
- Implementation revision 2, scope layout 2 (`_logoutCloser`, `_shutdownSubscription`), the `kitScopes.logout` connection slot and the `logout` trampoline. An upgrade over revision 1 migrates every addon scope in place and arranges the logout close of the open ones while it loads; a later revision keeps the subscriptions and the watcher without subscribing again. The executed code changed, so this is the first revision boundary of the 0.1.0 line: the upgrade specs now load the next revision instead of revision 2.
- 185 specs; `LogoutClose_spec.lua` covers the three cases that can arise, re-evaluation, a failure in LifecycleKit, the subscription disconnected by an early close, and the upgrades. The spec that left an addon scope open at logout until `CloseAddonScopes` now checks that the watcher closes it.

## 0.1.0 — 2026-09-23

First release: CommKit API generation 1, implementation revision 1.

- **Scopes.** `CreateScope`, `ForAddon` and `CloseAddonScopes` (which closes an addon's scope with pending sends cancelled as `"shutdown"`; LifecycleKit calls it at logout, and closing is terminal as in TimerKit); scopes offer `Register`, `Send`, `SyncSet`, `UnregisterAll`, `CancelAll`, `Close`, `IsClosed`, `GetAddonName`, `GetRegistrationCount` and `GetPendingCount`, and hold at most 32 registrations by default. A prefix is registered with the client on first use and the client's refusals are returned by name.
- **Wire protocol.** Every message starts with a control byte: `01` single, `02` first, `03` middle, `04` last, `05` abort; `06`–`7F` are reserved and refused. Chunks carry a stream id and a two-digit chunk number, written as bytes `80`–`FF` (radix 128) on the ordinary channel and as printable bytes `30`–`7A` (radix 75) on the logged channel, and 251 payload bytes; a message of up to 254 bytes is one chunk. Stream ids are assigned as first chunks leave and never reuse an id a stream of this client still in flight holds. A message cancelled, or failed by the client, after its first chunk left is followed by an abort, so receivers drop it at once and silently.
- **Sending.** `Send` queues into ALERT, NORMAL and BULK, served 4 : 2 : 1 per chunk, with per-destination pipes served round-robin inside each priority; `constraints.logged` selects `SendAddonMessageLogged`. Send handles offer `Cancel`, `GetState`, `GetBytesSent` and `GetBytesTotal`, with isolated progress and completion callbacks. The queue is bounded at 256 messages and 64 KB across every addon; a send that cannot be queued returns `nil` and `"queueFull"`, `"tooLarge"`, `"closed"`, `"forbiddenByte"`, `"badDistribution"` or `"unavailable"`, and allocates nothing.
- **Sender within the receiver's bounds.** A multi-chunk message starts only while this client's streams in flight, aborts included, stay within `maxInFlightPerSender` and `maxReassemblyBytesPerSender`; a message that alone declares more is refused as `"tooLarge"`, and one queued before `SetLimits` lowered that bound fails as `"tooLarge"`.
- **Budget.** One token bucket for the session (800 bytes per second, burst 4000, 40 bytes per message), halved below 20 frames per second (sampled on a TimerKit ticker only while something is queued), a tenth for five seconds after `PLAYER_ENTERING_WORLD`; throttle results, and results the client's enum does not name, set a pipe aside with doubling backoff; traffic other code sends is charged through HookKit secure hooks when HookKit is present.
- **Driver.** One SchedulerKit job at a time, existing only while something is queued: a send from a callback during a run is served by that run, a successor replaces any scheduled job, and emptying the queue, by sending or by cancelling, cancels the job and the ticker.
- **Reassembly.** Streams keyed by prefix, distribution, sender, channel and stream id, bounded in count (64), per-sender bytes (16 KB), per-sender streams (4) and time (30 s since the last chunk, on one TimerKit timer armed only while a stream is open); hostile headers are refused before anything is stored; streams of senders who left the group are evicted on `GROUP_ROSTER_UPDATE`; dropped streams are counted by reason and reported through the host error handler at most once per sender per minute. A single chunk is delivered without allocating a table.
- **SyncSet.** Named fields versioned by a 32-bit FNV-1a hash of a canonical encoding, exchanged as CodecKit frames (request, ack, deliver) with delta replies, optional per-field SchemaKit validation, a one-second reply interval per peer, requests answered only when whispered, one queued reply per peer and 8 KB of replies in all, a 64-peer cache, `OnChanged` with 16 listeners (all defaults, see *Limits*), and `Get`, `GetHash`, `GetRemote`, `Close` and `IsClosed`.
- **Package.** `GetQueueDepth`, `GetBudget`, `SetLimits`, `GetLimits` and `GetStatistics`. Queue and stream records are leased from PoolKit table pools. Every callback handed to another Kit resolves through a dispatch table, so a newer revision upgrades queued sends, open streams, registrations and SyncSets in place.
- **Secret values.** Secret arguments, constraints and limits raise at the caller before they are compared with anything; received messages with a secret argument are dropped before use.
- **Dependencies.** Registry API 2, SignalKit, EventKit, TimerKit, SchedulerKit and PoolKit API 1 are required, seven files with CommKit; CodecKit (required by `SyncSet`), HookKit and SchemaKit API 1 are optional. CommKit does not depend on LifecycleKit and does not subscribe to addon shutdown: `CloseAddonScopes` is the only close path for an addon scope (design constitution, principle 4b).
- **Limits.** `CommKit.UNBOUNDED`, one sentinel kept in the package state so every revision shares it. `CreateScope` and `ForAddon` accept `{ maxRegistrations }` (default 32, `UNBOUNDED` allowed) and scopes gain `GetMaxRegistrations`; `SyncSet` accepts `maxListeners` (default 16, `UNBOUNDED` allowed). The drop-report sender cap, the SyncSet peer cache and the SyncSet reply allowance become `SetLimits` entries `maxDropReportSenders` (64), `maxSyncPeers` (64) and `maxSyncReplyBytes` (8192). `SetLimits` refuses `UNBOUNDED` for every limit with a named reason: the queue is shared by every addon, reassembly, reports, peers and replies are grown by other players, and the budget values are rates. API.md gains a *Limits* table and lists the wire constants that stay fixed.
- **Tests.** 169 specs against a stubbed `C_ChatInfo` and the fixture clock; see `tests/README.md`.
