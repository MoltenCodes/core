# CodecKit Tests

The CodecKit suite covers:

- serialisation: the documented bytes of every scalar kind; round trips of `nil`, booleans, integers at every varint size up to ±2^53, integers past 2^53 as doubles, floats that do not survive `tostring`, both infinities, NaN, −0, the smallest and largest subnormals and normals; strings with every byte value, the escape byte, pipes and 64 KiB; the array, map and mixed layouts byte for byte; nested, sparse and table-keyed tables; holes; a table referenced twice; metatables ignored; `"unsupportedType"` and `"cycle"` refusals;
- frames: the header bytes of every stage combination, the print channel encoding the header, round trips through every combination of compression, channel and level, the `channel` decode assertion, unknown versions (binary and print), reserved and contradictory flags, pasted print text with wrapping and indentation, argument lists with `nil` in the middle and at the end, `Decode` against a list and `DecodeMany` against a single value, and the README export string;
- DEFLATE: fixed-Huffman output for tiny inputs checked against the RFC 1951 3.2.6 table, hand-built stored and fixed blocks, a dynamic stream made by zlib, round trips of text, binary, repetitive, random and all-zero input at levels 1, 6 and 9 and at every level, compression ratios and the bounded growth of incompressible input, malformed streams of every kind (block type 3, a bad stored complement, truncation, trailing data, a distance before any output, too many literal codes, an over-subscribed code), and inflation stopped at `maxOutputBytes`;
- channels: the exact addon escape table, all 256 byte values carried without NUL, line breaks or pipes (also inside whole frames), escape refusals; the 85-character print alphabet, known print vectors, round trips of every length from 0 to 40 with only alphabet characters, and print refusals;
- limits: the defaults, partial updates, fresh copies, refused values that change nothing, and each of `maxDepth`, `maxValues`, `maxStringLength` and `maxOutputBytes` on both the encoding and the decoding side, shared across a reload;
- malformed input: every malformed serialised shape with its reason, every proper prefix of a valid frame in every combination, 2000 random byte strings through all six decoding methods, 600 mutated frames and 500 random printable strings, all asserting that nothing raises and every reason is in the documented vocabulary;
- asynchronous variants: encode and decode under a 2 ms budget taking more than one frame and matching the synchronous result, failures handed to the callback, a cancelled job that never calls back and leaves the pool balanced, the missing-SchedulerKit refusal, scope and callback validation, and secret refusal at the caller;
- allocation guards (`collectgarbage("count")` with the collector stopped): no allocation when encoding a small table again on every channel or decoding scalar frames again, only the decoded tables on a small round trip, and a balanced, bounded pool after large encodes and refusals;
- secret values: refusal at the caller wherever the secret sits, for every string-taking method, a balanced pool after the refusal, and nothing treated as secret without `issecretvalue`;
- duplicate embedded loading, Registry publication, yielding to a newer revision, an in-place upgrade to revision 2 that keeps the limits, the pool and the wire format, a missing Registry, a missing PoolKit, and an incomplete facade;
- `error` levels: every receiver, option, argument, limit, secret and asynchronous failure reports the caller's own line;
- manifest/runtime API and revision consistency, the two required dependencies, and SchedulerKit declared optional after the top-level `api`.

`support/CodecKitTestEnv.lua` builds two environments on the shared fixture. The default one loads Registry, PoolKit and CodecKit without the World of Warcraft stubs, because CodecKit is pure Lua. `CodecKitTestEnv.Async` also loads SignalKit, EventKit, LifecycleKit, TimerKit and SchedulerKit; SchedulerKit is declared under `optionalDependencies`, so the runner puts it and its closure on `LUA_PATH`. `Async.NewPackageWithTickingClock` wraps `debugprofilestop` before SchedulerKit binds it, so every clock read advances CPU time and a frame budget runs out after a predictable amount of work.

The environment also provides an `issecretvalue` stub, a value that refuses every operation a secret refuses, a seeded generator for reproducible random input, deep equality that tells NaN and −0 apart, hexadecimal helpers, and `LoadRevision` for the upgrade spec.
