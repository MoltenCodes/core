# CodecKit Tests

The CodecKit suite covers:

- `Serialize_spec.lua`, serialisation: the documented bytes of every scalar kind; every NaN (negated, computed at run time, read back from sign-bit and payload bytes) written as the canonical quiet NaN, also by a copy whose sign test calls a NaN negative as Retail 12.1.0 b69933 did; round trips of `nil`, booleans, integers at every varint size up to ±2^53, integers past 2^53 as doubles, floats that do not survive `tostring`, both infinities, NaN, −0, the smallest and largest subnormals and normals; strings with every byte value, the escape byte, pipes and 64 KiB; the array, map and mixed layouts byte for byte; nested, sparse and table-keyed tables; holes; a table referenced twice; metatables ignored; `"unsupportedType"` and `"cycle"` refusals;
- `Frames_spec.lua`, frames: the header bytes of every stage combination, the print channel encoding the header, round trips through every combination of compression, channel and level, the `channel` decode assertion, unknown versions (binary and print), reserved and contradictory flags, pasted print text with wrapping and indentation, argument lists with `nil` in the middle and at the end, `Decode` against a list and `DecodeMany` against a single value, and the README export string;
- `Deflate_spec.lua`, DEFLATE: fixed-Huffman output for tiny inputs checked against the RFC 1951 3.2.6 table, hand-built stored and fixed blocks, a dynamic stream made by zlib, round trips of text, binary, repetitive, random and all-zero input at levels 1, 6 and 9 and at every level, compression ratios and the bounded growth of incompressible input, malformed streams of every kind (block type 3, a bad stored complement, truncation, trailing data, a distance before any output, too many literal codes, an over-subscribed code), and inflation stopped at `maxOutputBytes`;
- `Channels_spec.lua`, channels: the exact addon escape table, all 256 byte values carried without NUL, line breaks or pipes (also inside whole frames), escape refusals; the 85-character print alphabet, known print vectors, round trips of every length from 0 to 40 with only alphabet characters, and print refusals;
- `Limits_spec.lua`, limits: the defaults, partial updates, fresh copies, refused values that change nothing (out of range, another table, and `UNBOUNDED` for `maxDepth`, `maxOutputBytes` and `maxListValues`), each of `maxDepth`, `maxValues`, `maxStringLength` and `maxOutputBytes` on both the encoding and the decoding side, the `maxDepth` ceiling of 128 round-tripping, one `UNBOUNDED` sentinel kept across a reload, `maxValues` and `maxStringLength` lifted with `UNBOUNDED` on both sides and still bounded by `maxOutputBytes`, `maxListValues` raised to its 7900 ceiling and lowered, the default 4096-entry argument-list cap on both sides with a hostile 9000-entry list refused rather than raised, and the limits shared across a reload;
- `Malformed_spec.lua`, malformed input: every malformed serialised shape with its reason, every proper prefix of a valid frame in every combination, 2000 random byte strings through all six decoding methods, 600 mutated frames and 500 random printable strings, all asserting that nothing raises and every reason is in the documented vocabulary;
- `Async_spec.lua`, asynchronous variants: encode and decode under a 2 ms budget taking more than one frame and matching the synchronous result, failures handed to the callback, a cancelled job that never calls back and leaves the pool balanced, the call-time secret scan bounded by `maxOutputBytes` when `maxValues` is lifted, the missing-SchedulerKit refusal, scope and callback validation, and secret refusal at the caller, including a secret the encoder reaches only because it counts array elements once;
- `Allocation_spec.lua`, allocation guards (`collectgarbage("count")` with the collector stopped): no allocation when encoding a small table again on every channel or decoding scalar frames again, only the decoded tables on a small round trip, and a balanced, bounded pool after large encodes and refusals;
- `SecretValues_spec.lua`, secret values: refusal at the caller wherever the secret sits, for every string-taking method, for every option and limit value, a balanced pool after the refusal, and nothing treated as secret without `issecretvalue`;
- `Bootstrap_spec.lua`: duplicate embedded loading, Registry publication, yielding to a newer revision, an in-place upgrade from a revision 1 copy, from a revision 2 copy that keeps accepting absent options and limit values, from a revision 3 copy that writes the canonical NaN afterwards, from a revision 4 copy that writes the frames it wrote, from a revision 5 copy that compresses as it did (stored and Huffman blocks), from a revision 6 copy (loaded without the native path) that compresses natively afterwards, from a revision 7 copy (loaded without the probe of the native answer) that refuses a secret answer afterwards, from a revision 8 copy (loaded with `tostring` in the option refusal) that names a table key without running it afterwards and keeps its limits and frames, and to the next revision that keeps the limits (a lifted one and `maxListValues` included), the `UNBOUNDED` sentinel, the pool and the wire format, a missing Registry, a missing PoolKit, and an incomplete facade;
- `FastPath_spec.lua`, what revision 5 made cheaper, pinned to the wire: the frames revision 4 wrote written and decoded byte for byte, a reference writer matched on random values on every uncompressed channel, payloads of many thousand fragments and stage outputs folded into chunks, in-place decoding matching the staged decoders, no reference kept to encoded strings, a call nested through the secret probe and recovery from a probe that raises, malformed argument lists and stage limits on a body read in place, cycles refused only on the open path, and `maxDepth` refused one level past on both sides;
- `Native_spec.lua`, the client's compressor through a `C_EncodingUtil` stand-in: revision 6's bytes without it (and with a namespace that lacks the function), inputs of 64 bytes and more handed to `CompressString` with the Deflate method and shorter ones kept in Lua, the level mapping, the method and levels read from `Enum` or the documented numbers (also when an `Enum` field is a secret), native frames on every channel and argument lists decoded with the stand-in removed, Lua frames decoded with it present and `DecompressString` never called, zlib streams of every level as a client could return them, the fallback to Lua for a raise, nothing, a number, an empty string, a table and an oversized answer, the fallback for an answer the secret probe reports, the Lua refusal kept under a small `maxOutputBytes`, a secret refused before the stand-in sees it, no allocation for a short message, `EncodeAsync` native up to 256 KiB and Lua above, and `DecodeAsync` of a native frame;
- `ErrorLevels_spec.lua`, `error` levels: every receiver, option, argument, limit (an `UNBOUNDED` refusal and `maxListValues` included), secret (secret option and limit values, a secret receiver and a secret `options` argument included) and asynchronous failure reports the caller's own line;
- `KeyDescriptions_spec.lua`, key descriptions: a table key with a `__tostring` that records that it ran, given as an `Encode` or `Decode` option and as a `SetLimits` key, refused naming `<table>` with the metamethod never run; function, number, boolean and string keys; a secret option or limit key refused at the caller;
- `LoadCost_spec.lua`, what revision 9 builds on first use and caches: the byte text on the first encode, the print tables on the first print-safe encode or decode (and a print frame decoded first in a session), the DEFLATE tables on the first Lua compression or decompression, the clean-string cache (clean short strings recorded, strings that need escaping and long ones never, the 256 cap, a secret refused first), every addon-channel byte escaped and refused at the first, middle and last position, and the probe resolved at load kept or installed after load honoured;
- `Manifest_spec.lua`: manifest/runtime API and revision consistency, the two required dependencies, and SchedulerKit declared optional after the top-level `api`.

`support/CodecKitTestEnv.lua` builds two environments on the shared fixture. The default one loads Registry, PoolKit and CodecKit without the World of Warcraft stubs, because CodecKit is pure Lua. `CodecKitTestEnv.Async` also loads TimerKit and SchedulerKit; SchedulerKit is declared under `optionalDependencies`, so the runner puts it and its closure on `LUA_PATH`. `Async.NewPackageWithTickingClock` wraps `debugprofilestop` before SchedulerKit binds it, so every clock read advances CPU time and a frame budget runs out after a predictable amount of work.

The environment also provides an `issecretvalue` stub, a value that refuses every operation a secret refuses, a seeded generator for reproducible random input, deep equality that tells NaN and −0 apart, hexadecimal helpers, `LoadRevision` for the upgrade spec (optionally with one piece of source replaced, to model an older revision), `InstallEncodingUtil`, `RemoveEncodingUtil` and `SetEncodingGlobal`, a stand-in for the client's `C_EncodingUtil` whose `CompressString` writes stored blocks (or answers as a named failure mode) and records its calls, and whose `DecompressString` records a call CodecKit must never make, with `StoredDeflate`, the stand-in's encoder (both environments' `Reset` removes the stand-in and `Enum`), and `LoadPatched`, which loads the source with one piece of text replaced to model a host stock Lua cannot reproduce.
